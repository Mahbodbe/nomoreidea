Why this matters

Power-grid SCADA networks are wired directly to high-voltage breakers and substation equipment. A malicious packet can trip a line or damage hardware, so security has to be built into the network architecture from day one.

During summer 2026 at Modje Niroo R&D, I studied the full SCADA chain in Iran's power industry: PLCC, industrial Ethernet, serial and fibre links; RTU hardware; the dispatching hierarchy (SCC, AOC, RDC, DAS); the shift to IEC 61850 digital substations; and the domestic Paya-SCADA platform. I documented the work in a 189-page report: “SCADA Architecture and Security in the Iranian Power Industry”.

In the lab phase, I tested four detection scenarios on live traffic using Snort, Suricata, OSSEC, and Wazuh:

  • DNP3 Deep Packet Inspection: Detected analog point 3 crossing threshold 20 and separated breaker 11 open/close commands in both Snort and Suricata (offline PCAP and live).
  • SYN Flood Detection: Caught SYN floods using a 20 packets / 5 seconds behavioral threshold.
  • Brute-Force Correlation: Caught 21 consecutive failed login attempts to Paya-SCADA across both network and host layers.
  • Event-Rate Anomaly (SID 1000330): Flagged 9–10 events/sec spikes against a normal 15–25s interval, and verified that both DNP3 and WebSocket traffic ran in cleartext.

Terms used here

DNP3 / IEC 104
Protocols carrying analog measurements and breaker commands between substation RTUs and dispatch centers.
IEC 61850 (GOOSE)
Digital substation standard using fast Layer-2 multicast frames (EtherType 0x88B8) for protection events.
SID 1000330
Custom IDS rule written in the lab to catch sudden SCADA event-rate spikes.
